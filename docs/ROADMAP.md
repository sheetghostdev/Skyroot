# Skyroot: making a long playthrough worth it

Notes for the owner and for future Claude sessions.  Section 1 is measured; the
rest are proposals to discuss before building anything.

## 0. The archipelago (built)

The owner's pivot: keep the sky island as a second mode, and make the main game an
archipelago in the spirit of Stranded Deep, played with Vintage Story's mechanics,
with rafts like the game Raft.  Built so far:

- **Worlds**: 512 x 512 of sea, about eighteen islands of six kinds (home, atoll,
  wooded, rocky, marsh, meadow), each with things the others lack.  Far islands show
  on the horizon; only the blocks near you are meshed.
- **Water**: the sea is salt.  Coconuts, fresh ponds on some islands, and boiling
  sea water fresh in a pot over a fire; a waterskin carries four drinks.
- **Rafts**: built section by section on the water, ridden (you're carried, and can
  build on the deck), paddled, sailed with the wind, steered at a steering oar,
  anchored; they ground in the shallows.
- **Animals**: crabs, junglefowl (eggs), wild goats (hides); shy, huntable with a
  knapped spear; led on twine onto a raft and across the sea; fed in pairs they
  have young; hunted-out islands refill when no one is there.

How it answers section 1's worries: stone, flint, clay and wood now come from
different islands, so running low means a voyage, not a soft-lock; animals and
eggs are a renewable food that you can farm once you've brought a pair home.

### Next at sea (to discuss)

- **Fishing** (a line of twine and a bone/thorn hook from the raft; a fish trap in
  the shallows) and seaweed farming in the shallows.
- **Base on the raft**: storage jars and a fire pit (on a clay hearth) on deck, so a
  raft can be a real home; rafts that bump each other; mooring to a post.
- **Currents and weather at sea**: trade winds that change with the season, squalls
  that make sailing dangerous, a calm that makes paddling matter.
- **A chart**: the islands you've seen drawn into a map in the handbook (by sight,
  not handed to you), and landmarks (a beacon fire you build on a peak).
- **More island kinds**: a volcanic isle (obsidian for the best knapped edges, hot
  springs, later copper ore), a sea-stack with nesting birds, a wreck washed up
  after a storm with odd finds.
- **Sea life**: turtles laying eggs on the atolls, fish shoals, seabirds; sharks
  maybe, if it stays fun.
- **Animal care**: pens (fences need planks, so a saw), milking goats, shearing,
  feathers for fletching, leather from scraped and dried hides.

## 1. What the island holds today (fresh island, measured)

| Resource | Amount | Renews? | What it's for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Granite (+mossy) | 18,109 blocks | - | nothing yet: needs a pick that doesn't exist (**~80% of the island is locked**) |
| Soil (dirt + grass) | ~3,100 blocks | no | farming, building - it *is* the island |
| Blue clay | 313 blocks (≈1,250 lumps) | no | all pottery and fired bricks (≈ one modest brick house, minus pots) |
| Loose stones | 30 patches (≈45 stones) | no | every stone tool head |
| Flint | 6 nodules + 15% of 6 gravel (≈7) | no | the good tools |
| Sand / gravel | 11 / 6 blocks | no | almost nothing yet |
| Trees | ≈12 (74 logs) | yes (saplings) | firewood, logs; planks need a saw that doesn't exist |
| Water | 64 blocks (512 parts) | rain | drinking, stew, farming |
| Grass, flax, turnips, berries, mushrooms | small patches | yes | food, fibre, tinder, thatch |

Surface ≈ 1,000 columns (about 32 × 32).  Stone tools last 50-70 uses (flint ×1.7);
felling a tree costs an axe about 3 uses.

**What that means:** the first week is well supplied.  After that, three things
run out or stop:

1. **Tools are consumables made of a resource that doesn't renew.** About 45
   stones and 7 flint is maybe 50 tool heads.  When they're gone there's no axe,
   no knife (so no dry grass, so no tinder, so no fire).  That's a soft-lock,
   and it should be fixed whatever else we build.
2. **There is no next step after fired pottery.** The goals end around day 5.
   After that it's a sandbox with a short list of building blocks: soil, logs,
   thatch, bricks, and planks you can't make.
3. **Building has few materials and little nuance.** Clay for bricks competes
   with pottery and with the pond's banks; most of the stone is locked away.

## 2. What would make it feel great

Players love VS for its slow, physical, readable crafts, a ladder of eras
(stone → copper → bronze → iron), seasons that make you plan, and building you
can be proud of (chiselling).  They dislike grind without purpose, constant
hunger stress, losing items, and tools breaking for no reason.  A sky island
adds something VS doesn't have: **the island itself can be the project.**

Pillars:

- **Make the island yours**: shape it, grow it, and connect it to others.
- **A ladder of eras**, each new step a physical craft that builds on the last.
- **Seasons** that give medium-term goals (winter stores, firewood, warm clothes).
- **Expressive building**: many materials and fine detail.
- **Co-op projects** that need several hands and are visible from afar.

## 3. Ideas

### A. Fix the dead end first (small, high value)

- **Fire-setting** (a real ancient mining method): build a fire against a granite
  face, let it burn, then splash water on the hot rock (bowl or pot) and it
  cracks into loose granite chunks (stones).  This makes stone renewable and
  opens the 18,000-block core, with a cost in firewood and water and a risk of
  hollowing out your own island.
- **Flint in the core**: chalk seams with flint nodules found while fire-setting
  into the granite.  Sieving gravel in a reed basket gives the occasional flint.
- **Silt**: rain slowly lays clay on the pond floor (ties in with finite water),
  so the pond slowly gives clay back.

### B. The next era: copper (medium)

- **Ore veins in the core**: native copper and malachite showing on cracked faces.
- **Smelting**: crushed ore and charcoal in a clay crucible, fired in the pit
  kiln (or a later brick furnace).  Charcoal comes from a covered log pile
  (the charcoal pit is the same dig-and-cover idea as the pit kiln).
- **Casting in clay moulds**: moulds are shaped with the clay-forming grid you
  already know, then fired.  This gives copper pick, saw, chisel, hammer, knife
  and axe heads.
- **What copper unlocks**: the pick breaks granite outright, the saw cuts
  planks, slabs and stairs, the chisel enables detailed building (below), and
  metal tools last far longer and can be **repaired** rather than replaced.
- Later: **bronze** (tin from panning the pond's sediment, which also uses up
  water) and **sky iron** (below).

### C. Building you're proud of (medium-large)

- **Saw**: planks, stairs, slabs, doors, trapdoors, ladders, fences, shutters.
- **Chiselling** (VS's best-loved feature): carve any block on a 16³ grid.  We
  already render 16-cell grids for knapping and clay, so this is the same idea
  in 3D.  It makes arches, window frames, carved beams and statues possible.
- **More materials**: cut granite blocks and cobble (from fire-setting), glass
  (sand + a hotter kiln), coloured clays and glazes (plant dyes), lime plaster
  (burnt shells/stone), wattle and daub.
- **Furniture and decoration**: tables, shelves that display items (an extension
  of the ground storage), hanging lanterns, painted pots.

### D. Grow the island: the "skyroot" (the long goal)

A great root threads down through the island and holds it in the sky (the game's
name).  Feeding it water and compost over seasons makes it grow: new soil
creeps out from the edges, and small floating clods drift in and lodge against
the island.  Expanding the island would be the long, shared, visible goal (the
skyblock feeling, done the VS way, slowly and physically).  It also gives
compost, water management and farming a purpose beyond food.

### E. Seasons and the year (medium)

- Seasons that move the temperature and decide what grows.  Winter means
  planning: **firewood stacks** (a woodpile block), **preserved food** (drying
  racks, smoking over the fire pit, sealed jars), a **cellar** dug into the
  hill that stays cool.
- **Clothing**: flax → linen on a simple loom; warm layers for winter nights.
- Keep hunger gentle (VS players complain about constant food stress).  Winter
  should be about preparation, not starvation.

### F. Life and visitors (medium)

- **Birds** that nest on the island (eggs, feathers for arrows/bedding),
  **bees** (a straw skep gives honey and wax for candles and pollinates crops),
  fish in the pond if it's kept full.
- **Drifting islets**: every few days a small islet drifts past.  You have a
  day to bridge across before it leaves, with different trees, a sand bank,
  ore, or a nest.  Exploration without a map and without a story.
- **Falling stars**: rare meteorites that land on or near the island, carrying
  iron for the last era.
- **Storms**: lightning that can set a tree alight.  Rain collected by roofs
  into cisterns (finite water makes water engineering matter).

### G. Co-op projects and pride

- **Beacons and monuments**: a signal fire tower or kiln chimney that friends see
  from the menu backdrop and from drifting islets.  It needs several hands.
- **The island's chronicle**: a book (in the handbook) of what the group did:
  first fire, first copper, the day the island grew, the size of the brick hall.
  It records pride without making it a score.
- **Bigger crafts that want two people**: one works the bellows while the other
  pours the crucible; a two-person saw for big logs.

## 4. Suggested order

1. **Fire-setting + renewable stone/flint** (removes the soft-lock; small).
2. **Charcoal, ore, crucible, moulds, copper tools** (the next era; this is the
   biggest jump in "what's next").
3. **Saw-made blocks (planks, stairs, slabs, doors, ladders)**, then **chiselling**.
4. **Seasons + preservation + clothing.**
5. **The skyroot / island growth**, drifting islets, falling stars.

Each step should come with its handbook pages, goals, tests and a `--setup=`
scene, like the existing crafts.
